# Shakshuka

Eggs poached in a thick, spiced tomato and pepper sauce, cooked and served in one skillet. Breakfast, lunch, or dinner.

## Ingredients
- 3 tbsp [[olive-oil]]
- 1 large [[onion]], chopped
- 2 green [[bell-pepper]]s, chopped
- 2 cloves [[garlic]], chopped
- 1 tsp [[ground-coriander]], 1 tsp [[sweet-paprika]], 1/2 tsp [[ground-cumin]]
- a pinch of [[red-pepper-flakes]] (optional)
- [[kosher-salt]] and [[black-pepper]]
- 6 medium [[tomato]]es, chopped (about 6 cups)
- 1/2 cup [[tomato-sauce]]
- 6 large [[egg]]s
- 1/4 cup [[parsley]] and 1/4 cup [[mint]], chopped

## Method
1. Heat the oil in a large [[skillet]] over medium. When it shimmers, add the onion, peppers, garlic, the ground spices, and a pinch of salt and pepper. Cook, stirring now and then, until softened, about 5 minutes ([[blooming-spices]]).
2. Add the tomatoes and tomato sauce. Cover and simmer about 15 minutes, then uncover and cook until the sauce reduces and thickens. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
3. With a wooden spoon, make 6 well-spaced wells in the sauce and crack an egg into each ([[poaching-eggs-in-sauce]]).
4. Lower the heat to medium-low, cover, and cook until the whites are just set.
5. Off the heat, scatter over the parsley and mint. Add more black pepper or chile if you like. Serve with warm pita or crusty bread.

## Notes
- The sauce has to be thick before the eggs go in, or the wells collapse and you get scrambled eggs in soup (per [[shakshuka-recipe-mediterranean-dish]]).
- Herbs go on after the heat is off, so they stay fresh.
- **Make ahead:** the sauce keeps overnight in the fridge. Reheat it in the skillet and pick up at step 3. Leftovers with the eggs in only keep 1-2 days.
- **With meat:** brown 1/2 lb ground beef or lamb in olive oil, season, set aside, wipe the skillet, make the sauce, and return the meat to simmer with the tomatoes before adding the eggs.
- The source gives prep 10 min and cook 30 min but states total time as 30; 40 minutes is the honest total.
- The source lists servings as "6 people (up to)" - it is 6 eggs, so 6 is the ceiling rather than a firm yield.
