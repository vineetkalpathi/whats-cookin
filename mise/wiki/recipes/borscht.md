# Borscht

Beet and cabbage soup built on caramelized onions and tomato paste, finished with red wine vinegar, sour cream and dill. Attributed to Kumar.

## Ingredients
- 2-4 tbsp [[olive-oil]], or neutral oil
- 1 [[onion]], large, chopped
- 2-3 clove [[garlic]], minced
- 2 [[bay-leaf]], called tej patta in the source
- 1 [[tomato]]
- 2-3 tbsp [[tomato-paste]], one very small can
- 1 [[russet-potato]], large, peeled and cut into 1/2-inch cubes
- 2 [[carrot]], peeled and shredded
- 2 stem [[celery]], chopped into cubes (optional)
- 2 cup [[green-cabbage]], thinly sliced
- 2 [[scallion]], chopped; the source says leek or spring onions
- 2 [[beet]], large, peeled and shredded
- [[dill]], chopped — half into the pot, the rest as garnish
- 8 cup [[vegetable-broth]], 2 quarts; the source also allows meat broth
- 2 tsp [[vegetable-bouillon-paste]], in place of the broth
- 3-4 tbsp [[red-wine-vinegar]]
- 1-2 tbsp [[black-pepper]], freshly ground and crushed
- [[kosher-salt]], to taste
- [[red-pepper-flakes]], to taste (optional)
- 1 cup [[sour-cream]], 8 oz, a dollop per bowl
- [[rye-bread]], toasted, to serve
- [[butter]], for the bread

## Method
1. Prep everything first: chop the onion, mince the garlic, chop the spring onions and celery, peel and cube the potato, peel and shred the beets and carrots, slice the cabbage thinly, chop the dill.
2. Heat the oil in a [[dutch-oven]]. Add the garlic, bay leaves and onion.
3. Cook the onions until slightly brown — this is the step the author says sets the recipe apart ([[caramelizing-onions]]).
4. Stir in the tomato paste with the chopped tomato and cook until the mixture leaves its oil.
5. Add the potato, carrot and celery, and cook, stirring.
6. When the potato is about 30% cooked, add the cabbage, spring onions, half the dill and the shredded beets.
7. Mix and sauté another 5 minutes. Add the crushed red pepper now if you want it spicy.
8. Add the broth, or the bouillon dissolved in water. Boil, cover, and simmer.
9. Taste and salt lightly — the broth is already salted.
10. Simmer until the vegetables are cooked to a bite ([[saute-to-bite]]).
11. Add the red wine vinegar and the crushed black pepper, and boil a minute or two.
12. Serve each bowl with a dollop of sour cream and dill on top, with toasted buttered rye or pumpernickel.

## Notes
- **Attributed to Kumar**, and titled "Borcht – Russian Soup" in the source. The page keeps the conventional spelling; the original's is in `aliases`.
- **Caramelizing the onions is the author's stated point of difference** — "the one different thing".
- Second cue, familiar from the Indian pages: cook the onion and tomato paste until it "leaves oil" and the fat separates.
- Vegetables are staged: potato, carrot and celery first; cabbage, spring onion, dill and beets only once the potato is about 30% done, so the beets keep their colour.
- Salt late and lightly.
- Heat goes in early — crushed red pepper during the sauté, not at the table.
- Dill is used twice: half into the pot, the rest as garnish. Sour cream is a garnish, never stirred into the pot.
- **Vinegar quantity is contradictory:** the list says 3-4 tbsp, the method says "2/3 tbs", read as 2 or 3. Start at 2 and taste.
- **The fresh tomato is listed but never explicitly added**; the phrase about the onion-and-tomato mix leaving oil puts it with the tomato paste.
- Celery is marked optional because the source's list says so, though the method adds it unconditionally.
- Meat broth is offered as a straight swap, which would make it non-vegetarian. Without the bread and with a gluten-free bouillon, the soup itself is gluten-free.
- The method's last sentence has no terminal punctuation and sits at the bottom of the text block; page 2 is photos only, so nothing continues, but a trailing clause may have been lost.

### Scaling
- Built around 2 quarts of broth and halves cleanly: 1 quart broth, 1 beet, 1 carrot, 1 cup cabbage, a small potato.
- Keeps and reheats well, and tastes better the next day.
