# Instant Pot Mexican Rice

Restaurant-style Mexican rice: toasted long grain rice pressure-cooked with tomato sauce and tomato bouillon, with carrot and peas. The standard side for anything Mexican.

## Ingredients
- 1 1/2 cups [[long-grain-white-rice]], rinsed and drained
- 1/4 cup [[neutral-oil]]
- 1 tsp [[garlic]], minced
- 1/4 medium [[onion]], finely diced
- 1/4 cup [[tomato-sauce]] (or 2 pureed tomatoes)
- 2 [[tomato-bouillon]] cubes, finely chopped (or 2 tsp granulated)
- 1/4 tsp [[kosher-salt]]
- 1 [[carrot]], diced
- 1 1/2 cups water
- 1/3 cup [[frozen-peas]]

## Method
1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then drain well.
2. Set the [[instant-pot]] to saute. Once hot, add the oil.
3. Add the rice and saute, stirring often, until lightly golden, about 5 minutes ([[toasting-rice]]).
4. Add the garlic, onion, tomato sauce, salt, bouillon, and carrot, and stir.
5. Add the water and stir well, making sure the bouillon is dissolved. Turn the pot off.
6. Seal the lid and valve. Cook on Manual / High Pressure for 3 minutes (tender to the bite) or 4 (softer) ([[pressure-cooking]]).
7. Let the pressure release naturally for 10 minutes.
8. Open, add the peas, and fluff gently with a fork - don't stir. Rest a few minutes under the lid before serving.

## Notes
- The 1:1 rice-to-water ratio is right for pressure cooking; nothing evaporates under the lid (per [[instant-pot-authentic-mexican-rice]]).
- Toasting the drained rice to golden is what makes this taste like restaurant rice rather than tomato rice.
- Don't quick-release, and don't stir after cooking - both break the grains.
- **Tomato bouillon** carries the flavour and the colour. Chicken broth can stand in, but the rice won't have the same colour. Most supermarket tomato bouillon (Knorr Caldo de Tomate and similar) contains chicken, so check the label if this needs to be vegetarian - `diet` here is marked gluten-free only for that reason.
- The stated 23 minutes doesn't count the time the pot takes to come up to pressure, so budget closer to 35.
- There is a stovetop version on the same site, linked from the source page.
