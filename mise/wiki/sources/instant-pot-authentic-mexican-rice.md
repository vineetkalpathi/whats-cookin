# Instant Pot Authentic Mexican Rice (Tastes Better From Scratch)

Pressure-cooker version of Mexican restaurant rice: long grain white rice toasted in oil, then
cooked with tomato sauce, tomato bouillon, onion, garlic and carrot, with peas folded in at the
end. 6 servings, 23 minutes total. The author says she developed it while living in Mexico and
adapted it to the Instant Pot.

Captured with Obsidian Web Clipper after the site returned HTTP 403 to both `capture.py` and a
direct web fetch.

## Key takeaways
- **Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then drain well.** Surface starch is what makes
  pressure-cooked rice gluey.
- **Toast the drained rice in oil on saute until lightly golden, about 5 minutes.** This is the
  step that separates this from plain tomato rice - it sets the nutty flavour and keeps grains
  separate.
- **Tomato bouillon is the flavour backbone**, not just salt: 2 cubes (or 2 tsp granulated),
  finely chopped so they dissolve. Chicken broth can stand in but the rice loses its colour.
- **1 1/2 cups rice to 1 1/2 cups water** - a 1:1 ratio, much less liquid than stovetop, because
  nothing evaporates under pressure.
- **3 minutes at high pressure for rice tender to the bite, 4 for softer**, then a full 10-minute
  natural release. Don't quick-release.
- **Peas go in after cooking**, with the residual heat doing the work. Fluff with a fork, never
  stir, then rest a few minutes under the lid.
