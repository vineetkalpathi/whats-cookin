# Red Wine Vinegar

Red wine soured into vinegar. Sharp and fruity with a faint tannic edge, less brutal than white vinegar and nothing like the sweetness of balsamic. Its job is to cut sweetness. Beets go very sweet when they are cooked, and the spoonful stirred into [[borscht]] at the very end, off the heat, is what makes the pot taste like borscht rather than sweet vegetable soup. Added early it boils off and the soup tastes dull again.

**Cuisines:** [[russian-ukrainian]]

## Choosing & storing
The cheapest bottles taste thin and purely acidic; a mid-range one is worth the small difference. The cupboard is fine and it keeps indefinitely. Sediment, or a cloudy jellyish "mother" floating in the bottle, is harmless. Acidity runs around 6 to 7% but varies by brand, so taste before deciding how much a dish needs.

## Substitutes
- [[lemon]]: 1:1 juice, brighter and fruitier, without the tannic depth
- [[rice-vinegar]]: 1:1, milder and sweeter, so you need a little more
