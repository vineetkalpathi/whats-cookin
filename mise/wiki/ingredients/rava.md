# Rava

Coarsely milled durum wheat, sold as rava or sooji and called semolina in English. Sandy and granular rather than powdery, with a plain wheaty taste. Its job is to soak up moisture and then set, which gives a batter body. In [[methi-lagaan]] it goes in dry and hydrates in the moisture the vegetables release, with no water added. It is a grind, not a flour: [[whole-wheat-flour]] is milled fine and develops gluten into a dough, and [[rice-flour]] is pure starch used for crispness, so neither stands in for rava.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Fine rava (often sold as bombay rava) is what these batters want; coarse idli rava is a different grind and stays gritty. Buy small bags. The germ makes it go stale within a few months, and it is a favourite of weevils, so keep it airtight and in the fridge or freezer in a warm kitchen. Stale rava smells sour or musty before it tastes of anything.

## Prep notes
- Give a rava mixture 10 to 15 minutes to hydrate before it hits the pan. Cooked straight away it stays sandy in the mouth.
- Dry-roasting it until it smells nutty deepens the flavour and keeps grains separate, though [[methi-lagaan]] does not ask for it.
