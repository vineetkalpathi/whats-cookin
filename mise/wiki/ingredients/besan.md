# Besan

Flour milled from split brown chickpeas, the same pulse as [[chana-dal]]. It is nutty, a little bitter raw, and gluten-free, so unlike [[all-purpose-flour]] it binds through starch and protein rather than by developing structure: batters cling and set but never go elastic. That makes it the base for fritter batters such as [[killu-bonda]] and the body of [[methi-lagaan]]. Note that a Western packet labelled "chickpea flour" is sometimes milled from whole white chickpeas, which is coarser and blander than besan proper.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Buy it in modest bags from [[indian-grocery]] rather than a large sack. It carries more fat than wheat flour and goes stale faster, and stale besan smells musty and tastes sharply bitter in a way that frying only concentrates. Airtight and cool, or in the fridge if the kitchen is warm. It cakes into hard lumps, so sieve it before use.

## Prep notes
Add water gradually and whisk to a smooth batter, then rest it ten to fifteen minutes so the flour hydrates. Raw besan tastes chalky, so either toast it gently first or make sure the batter cooks right through; a fritter that is golden outside and pasty inside has not had long enough.

## Substitutes
- [[rice-flour]]: up to a third of the besan, crisper crust but much less binding
- [[all-purpose-flour]]: 1:1 for binding only, loses the nutty flavour entirely
