# Sweet Corn

Sweet yellow corn kernels, bought fresh on the cob, frozen or canned. Mild and sugary with a pop of juice; it contributes sweetness and texture rather than depth. In [[tex-mex-salad]] and [[pasta-salad]] it goes in cold and raw, and in [[methi-lagaan]] it is cooked into the mixture, where the sweetness balances the bitterness of the methi.

## Choosing & storing
Frozen is the sensible default: it is picked and frozen quickly and beats a tired cob or a canned tin for most of the year. Fresh cobs should feel heavy, with a bright green moist husk and a pale, damp cut stem. Corn sugar turns to starch within days of picking, so buy cobs the day you want them and keep them cold until then. Canned corn is softer and usually salted or sweetened, so drain and rinse it. One cob gives roughly ¾ cup of kernels.

## Prep notes
- For the cold salads, frozen corn only needs thawing, which a rinse under warm water does in a minute. Do not boil it. Pat it dry or it waters down the dressing.
- Charring thawed corn in a dry hot pan until it spots brown adds a lot to anything Tex-Mex, though neither recipe asks for it.
