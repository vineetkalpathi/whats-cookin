# Baking Powder

Bicarbonate of soda pre-mixed with a dry acid and a little starch, so it raises on its own. That is the whole difference from [[baking-soda]], which needs acid from somewhere else in the batter. Most tins are double-acting: one fizz on contact with liquid, a second in the oven. In [[almond-flour-cake]] it backs up the eggs in a batter with no gluten to hold the gas, and in [[methi-lagaan]] it keeps the mixture from baking tight.

## Choosing & storing
It loses strength within a few months of opening, and a flat cake is usually a dead tin rather than a bad recipe. Test it by stirring half a teaspoon into hot water: it should froth up at once, not trickle. Keep the lid tight and only ever put a dry spoon in, as a damp one sets off the whole tin from the inside. Write the opening date on the lid.

## Substitutes
- [[baking-soda]] with cream of tartar: 1/4 tsp soda to 1/2 tsp cream of tartar per 1 tsp powder, acts once so get it in the oven straight away (no cream of tartar page yet)
