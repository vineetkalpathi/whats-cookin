# Coconut Cream

The thick fraction of coconut milk: the same ingredient with less water, around 20 to 25% fat against roughly 10% for [[coconut-milk]]. It is for richness at the end of a dish rather than for simmering in, which is why [[thai-curry]] buys both and adds both, milk for the body of the sauce and cream to finish it. Not to be confused with the sweetened creamed coconut sold for cocktails, which is sugar syrup.

**Cuisines:** [[thai]]

## Choosing & storing
The ingredients should be coconut and water, perhaps with guar gum, and nothing else; anything labelled "light" is simply more water. Separation in the tin is normal and usually a sign of a good one. A grey tinge or a sour smell on opening means the tin has turned, and a bulging or rusted tin goes in the bin unopened. Leftovers keep three or four days in a tub in the fridge, or freeze; the texture breaks on thawing, which does not matter once it is cooked into something.

## Prep notes
Add it off a hard boil and do not let it rip after that, as sustained boiling splits the fat out as oil. Chilling the tin firms the cap so you can scoop the solid cream and leave the water behind; otherwise stir it smooth before measuring.

## Substitutes
- [[coconut-milk]]: 1:1 but thinner, so reduce the sauce longer or scoop the solid layer off a chilled tin
