# Curry Leaves

Small glossy leaves on a thin stem, with a citrus-and-savoury smell that only really comes out when
they hit hot fat. Nothing to do with curry powder, and not the same kind of ingredient as
[[bay-leaf]]: where a bay leaf is a background note dropped into a long simmer and fished out,
curry leaves are fried for seconds at the start of a tadka and left in the dish to be eaten. They
go into the hot oil in [[yellow-moong-khichdi]] and chopped raw into the batter in [[killu-bonda]].

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Look for sprigs with firm, deep-green leaves; limp or yellowing ones have little smell left. They
die fast — three or four days loose in the fridge. Strip the leaves off the stems, wrap them dry in
kitchen paper in a sealed box, and they will hold a week or so. Freezing is the honest answer for a
large bunch from an [[indian-grocery]]: the leaves lose their crispness but keep most of the aroma,
and they go straight into the tadka from frozen. Dried curry leaves are nearly scentless and not
worth buying.

## Prep notes
Dry them well before frying — wet leaves spit violently in hot oil. They sputter and curl within a
few seconds, which is when the aroma releases; much longer and they blacken and taste acrid.
