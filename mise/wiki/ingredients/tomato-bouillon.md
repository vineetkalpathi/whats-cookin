# Tomato Bouillon

Concentrated tomato-and-salt seasoning sold as cubes or granules (Knorr Caldo de Tomate is the common brand). It carries both the savoury backbone and the orange-red colour of restaurant Mexican rice - this is the ingredient that makes it taste the way it does.
> [!warning] Usually contains chicken
> Despite the name, most supermarket tomato bouillon lists chicken fat or chicken powder. Check the label if the dish needs to be vegetarian. This is why [[instant-pot-mexican-rice]] is tagged gluten-free but not vegetarian.

**Cuisines:** [[mexican]]

## Choosing & storing
Look in the Latin or Mexican aisle rather than with the stock cubes. Granulated is easier to measure (2 tsp per 2 cubes); cubes need chopping finely so they dissolve.

Availability is marked `specialty`: a full-range supermarket with a Latin aisle will have it, but smaller stores may not. `find_at` is deliberately empty - no store has been confirmed yet. Fill it in after a shop rather than guessing.

## Substitutes
- chicken or vegetable broth: replace the water 1:1 and drop the cubes, but the rice loses its colour (per [[instant-pot-authentic-mexican-rice]])
