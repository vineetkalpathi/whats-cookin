# Tamarind

The pulp of the tamarind pod, sold as a sticky dark block of seedless pulp, as a smooth jarred concentrate, or as whole pods. Intensely sour with a raisin-and-date fruitiness underneath, which is what distinguishes it from [[lime]]: tamarind is sourness with body and sweetness behind it. It is the souring agent in [[onion-tomato-chutney]], and the base of jarred [[sweet-chutney]].

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
The block tastes better and costs less but has to be soaked and strained. Concentrate is instant, flatter and much stronger, so start with about a quarter as much by volume and taste. Thai jars are thinner and milder than Indian concentrate, so never trust a spoon measure across brands. The block keeps a year or more wrapped in the fridge, hardening as it goes, which does it no harm; opened concentrate keeps months refrigerated.

## Prep notes
- Soak a lime-sized lump of block in ½ cup hot water for 15 to 20 minutes, squash it apart with your fingers, then push it through a sieve. Discard the fibre, skin and any stones.
- Add it towards the end and taste as you go. It is very easy to overshoot, and the acid also stops dal and pulses from softening any further, so it should go in after they are tender.

## Substitutes
- [[lime]]: about 1 tbsp juice per 1 tsp concentrate, sharper and cleaner, missing the fruity depth
- [[amchur]]: about ½ tsp powder per 1 tsp concentrate, sour and green-tasting, adds no colour or sweetness
