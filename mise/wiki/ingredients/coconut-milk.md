# Coconut Milk

Full-fat coconut milk from a can: sweet, rich and faintly grassy, and the thing that gives a Thai
curry its body as well as its fat. Thinner and more pourable than [[coconut-cream]] — [[thai-curry]]
uses both, the milk for volume and the cream for richness, added together at the end.

**Cuisines:** [[thai]]

## Choosing & storing
Buy it in cans, full fat, and not the carton "coconut beverage", which is mostly water. The label
should read coconut and water; a little guar gum is normal. Separation in the can is normal too,
not a fault. Unopened cans keep a year or more in the cupboard. Once opened it sours quickly —
decant into a tub and use within three or four days. It freezes, but splits on thawing, which is
fine for cooking.

## Prep notes
It splits if it boils hard, so it goes in late and barely cooks. In
[[thai-coconut-butternut-squash-soup]] it goes in off the heat after blending, with a few
tablespoons held back to drizzle and swirl on top of each bowl. Stir the can smooth before
measuring unless the recipe wants the thick cream separately.

## Substitutes
- [[coconut-cream]]: thin with water, about 2 parts cream to 1 part water, for something close to coconut milk
- light coconut milk: 1:1, noticeably thinner and less rich (no page yet)
