# Almonds

Whole raw almonds, bought to be toasted. Raw they are milky and a bit dull; toasted they turn sweet and properly nutty, and that is most of what they contribute. In [[asian-cabbage-salad]] they go in at the very last moment, so they stay crisp instead of softening in the dressing.

## Choosing & storing
Buy raw rather than pre-roasted and salted, so you control both the toast and the salt. Nuts are high in fat and go rancid quietly: the smell is of old paint and the taste is bitter at the back of the mouth. Buy where stock turns over quickly, in quantities you will finish, and keep them in the freezer if that will take months.

## Prep notes
Toast in a dry pan over medium heat for five to eight minutes, or in the oven at 160C for ten, until they smell fragrant and the skins crack. They carry on cooking in the residual heat, so tip them straight out of the pan. Flaked or slivered almonds burn in half the time. Cool before chopping, or they go oily.

## Substitutes
- [[peanuts]]: 1:1, softer and sweeter, less of a clean snap
- cashews: 1:1, richer and much less crunchy once dressed (no page yet)
