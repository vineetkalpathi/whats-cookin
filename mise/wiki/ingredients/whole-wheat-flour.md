# Whole Wheat Flour

Atta: wheat milled with the bran and germ left in, so it is beige, slightly nutty and faintly bitter, and thirstier than white flour. In the [[killu-bonda]] batter it does the binding and gives the fritters their wheaty body, while [[rice-flour]] does the crisping. Indian atta is milled finer and from a softer wheat than Western wholemeal; both work, but wholemeal gives a slightly coarser batter and usually wants a touch more water. Coarser again is [[rava]], which is a grind rather than a flour and cannot stand in for either.

## Choosing & storing
The germ carries oil, so wholewheat flour goes rancid within a few months where white flour lasts a year. Buy bags small enough to finish, smell for a sour or crayon-like note before using, and keep it in the fridge or freezer in a warm kitchen. Sieve out the lumps it forms when it picks up damp.

## Prep notes
- It absorbs water slowly. Let a wholewheat batter stand ten minutes and it will have tightened noticeably, so adjust the consistency then rather than straight after mixing.

## Substitutes
- [[all-purpose-flour]]: 1:1, lighter and less nutty, and needs slightly less water
