# Almond Flour

Blanched almonds ground fine and sieved. No gluten, a lot of fat, faintly sweet, so it behaves more like a wet ingredient than a dry one: batters stay loose and bake dense and moist. The alias "ground almonds" hides a real difference, as almond meal is milled with the skins on and left coarser, giving a speckled, heavier crumb. Either works in [[almond-flour-cake]], which has no wheat flour at all and takes its lift from eggs, but the fine flour makes the better sponge.

## Choosing & storing
Buy small bags and smell them. Good almond flour smells of marzipan; rancid flour smells faintly of paint or crayon and turns the whole cake bitter. The fridge or freezer is the right place for it, in a sealed bag, and it is fine to use straight from cold. It absorbs moisture and sets into lumps, so sieve or whisk before measuring.

## Substitutes
- ground almonds or almond meal: 1:1, coarser and darker crumb (no page yet)
- [[all-purpose-flour]]: not a substitute in either direction; the fat and lack of gluten make it a different kind of batter
