# Chana Dal

Split, skinned brown chickpeas. The same species as [[chickpeas]] but a different type (desi rather than kabuli), so the grains are smaller, nuttier and sweeter, and being split they cook without the long soak a whole chickpea needs. Ground, this is [[besan]]. In [[onion-tomato-chutney]] it is not being used as a pulse at all: a spoonful fried in oil until golden acts as a toasted, nutty thickener.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Uniform pale yellow grains with few broken pieces and no stones. Age is the thing to watch, because very old dal looks dull and chalky and simply refuses to soften however long you cook it, which is unrecoverable mid-recipe. Buy modest quantities from [[indian-grocery]], keep it airtight away from heat, and it is good for a year. Check for weevils if a bag has been open a while.

## Prep notes
Rinse and pick it over first. When frying it for a tadka or chutney, take it to an even gold: pale is raw and chalky, a shade past gold is bitter, and the window is maybe thirty seconds. As a dal, soak thirty minutes then simmer twenty to twenty-five, or around eight minutes under pressure.

## Substitutes
- [[urad-dal]]: 1:1 when frying for a chutney or tadka, earthier and softer when cooked
- [[yellow-moong-dal]]: 1:1, cooks faster and breaks down sooner, much less nutty
