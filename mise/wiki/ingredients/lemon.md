# Lemon

The standard sour citrus: sharp and rounded, and much less perfumed than [[lime]]. Either will do
where the job is simply acid, but they are not interchangeable in a dish built on one of them. Here
it is only ever the juice — a tablespoon in the shaken dressing for [[quinoa-salad]], and juice with
olive oil as the base layer of [[pasta-salad]].

## Choosing & storing
Pick lemons that feel heavy with thin, slightly glossy skin; the big thick-skinned ones look
generous and yield little. One lemon gives roughly 2-3 tbsp of juice. A few days at room
temperature is fine, weeks in the fridge, longer in a sealed bag. A cut half dries out fast — juice
the whole thing and keep the juice instead.

## Prep notes
Roll it firmly on the counter and let it come to room temperature before juicing; both get more out
of it. Zest it before juicing if you want zest at all: the aroma is in the peel, the juice is mostly
acid.

## Substitutes
- [[lime]]: 1:1, more aromatic and a little more bitter
- [[red-wine-vinegar]]: start at about two-thirds the volume, acid without any citrus aroma
