# Butternut Squash

Dense orange winter squash, sweet and slightly nutty, which is why it carries coconut and chilli so well. In [[thai-coconut-butternut-squash-soup]] it goes in raw and simmers in the liquid rather than being roasted first. That is quicker and gives a cleaner, brighter squash flavour, at the cost of the caramelised depth roasting would add.

**Cuisines:** [[american]]

## Choosing & storing
Pick one heavy for its size with matte, hard skin you cannot dent with a thumbnail, and a dry intact stem. Shiny skin means it was picked early and will taste watery. Soft patches, splits or mould around the stem mean it will not keep. Whole, it sits happily in a cool dry cupboard for a month or more and does not want the fridge. Once cut, wrap and refrigerate it and use within a few days, as the cut faces dry out and go fibrous.

## Prep notes
Top and tail it, halve it lengthways, scoop the seeds, then peel with a knife rather than a peeler. A couple of minutes in the microwave softens the skin if it is fighting you. Cut even chunks of two or three centimetres so they finish together; smaller dice collapses, which is no loss in a soup that gets blended.

## Substitutes
- kabocha or pumpkin: 1:1, kabocha is drier and sweeter, pumpkin wetter and blander (no page yet)
- [[carrot]]: 1:1 in a blended soup, sweeter and far less starchy, so use less liquid
