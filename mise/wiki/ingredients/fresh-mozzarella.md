# Fresh Mozzarella

Soft white mozzarella sold in balls in whey or brine: milky, mild, barely salty and wet.
Buffalo-milk mozzarella di bufala is richer and slightly tangier than cow's-milk fior di latte.
It is a different ingredient from the low-moisture block sold for pizza — fresh mozzarella softens
and slumps rather than browning and stretching, which is what [[tomato-mozzarella-panini]] wants,
sliced into rounds the same thickness as the tomato.

**Cuisines:** [[italian]]

## Choosing & storing
It should be sitting in liquid, and should feel heavy and yielding; anything rubbery or dry-surfaced
has been out of its brine too long. Keep it in its own liquid and eat it within a few days of
opening — it sours quickly and the texture turns squeaky. Do not freeze it. Vacuum-packed slices are
drier, and easier to layer.

## Prep notes
Slice it and let the rounds drain on kitchen paper for a few minutes before they go into a
sandwich, or the bread goes soggy. It needs only enough heat to soften.

## Substitutes
- low-moisture mozzarella: 1:1, drier and stretchier; it browns instead of slumping, so a better melt but a less milky sandwich (no page yet)
