# Idli Batter

Fermented batter of ground rice and [[urad-dal]], sold in tubs and normally steamed into idlis.
It is mildly sour from the ferment, thick, and full of the gas the fermentation produced — which is
why it works as both binder and leavener in [[killu-bonda]], where two scoops go into the
four-flour batter. The tang is what a plain-flour version misses.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Sold refrigerated at an [[indian-grocery]], usually in a dated tub. It keeps fermenting in the
fridge, so an older tub is sourer and has less lift left. It should smell pleasantly sour, not sharp
or alcoholic. It can rise enough to push the lid off, so leave room. Use it within a few days of
opening, keep it cold, and let it come up to room temperature before relying on it to leaven
anything. It freezes, with some loss of lift.

## Prep notes
Stir it gently — beating it knocks out the gas you bought it for. In [[killu-bonda]] the batter
also takes baking soda or eno just before frying, so the idli batter's own lift is a bonus rather
than the whole leavening.

## Substitutes
- [[all-purpose-flour]]: with [[rice-flour]], 1/3 cup of each in place of two scoops — the source's own fallback for [[killu-bonda]]; no tang and no lift
