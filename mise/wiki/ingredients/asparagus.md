# Asparagus

Spring spears, grassy and faintly bitter, at their sweetest within a day or two of cutting. Here it is a soup vegetable: [[asparagus-soup]] simmers and blends it, so flavour matters more than looks and thin or thick spears both work. Worth knowing that the source for that recipe discards the tips along with the woody ends, which is unusual, as most cooks keep the tips for being the tenderest part.

## Choosing & storing
Look for tight closed tips and stalks that feel firm and squeak against each other. The cut ends should be moist rather than dried and split. Tips going dark, soft and slightly slimy mean it is old, and limp spears will not come back. Stand them upright in a centimetre of water in the fridge, or wrap the cut ends in a damp cloth. Use within two or three days; the sugars drop fast and it starts tasting woody.

## Prep notes
Snap or cut off the bottom where it stops being tender, usually the lower third. Thick spears are worth peeling below the tip. For soup there is no need to be precious about even lengths. Do not let it overcook in anything green-looking, as it goes khaki and mushy.

## Substitutes
- [[broccoli]]: 1:1 by weight in a blended soup, sweeter and more sulphurous
- frozen asparagus: 1:1 in soup, fine for blending, far too limp for anything else (no page yet)
