# Dill

Feathery fresh fronds, grassy and faintly aniseed, with a sharpness that cuts fat and sweetness.
It is a finishing herb more than a cooking one, because heat flattens it within minutes — which is
exactly why [[borscht]] uses it twice, half into the pot and the rest chopped over each bowl with
the sour cream. [[quinoa-salad]] offers it as an alternative to [[parsley]], not alongside it.

**Cuisines:** [[russian-ukrainian]]

## Choosing & storing
Buy bunches that are bright green and still perky; dill wilts and yellows faster than almost any
other herb. Stand the stems in a glass of water with a bag over the top, or wrap it in damp kitchen
paper in a box — a week at best either way. Slimy or yellowed fronds are finished. It freezes
acceptably once chopped. Dried dill is a different, hay-like thing: usable in a pot, no good as the
garnish.

## Prep notes
Use the fronds. The thicker stems are too tough to eat but worth throwing into a simmering pot and
pulling out later. Chop it just before serving — cut dill bruises and loses its smell sitting
around.

## Substitutes
- [[parsley]]: 1:1 as the chopped green finish, loses the aniseed note entirely; named as the alternative in [[quinoa-salad]]
- dried dill: 1 tsp per tbsp fresh, fine stirred into a soup, no use as a garnish (no page yet)
