# Rice Flour

Raw white rice milled fine. More or less tasteless and almost pure starch, with no gluten at all, so it cannot build structure. What it does is fry crisp and stay crisp. In the [[killu-bonda]] batter the [[whole-wheat-flour]] gives body and the rice flour gives the shell, which is the difference between a crunchy fritter and a bready one. Do not substitute glutinous or sweet rice flour (mochiko, shiratamako): that is a different rice and turns sticky and chewy.

## Choosing & storing
Plain white rice flour from an Indian or Thai brand is fine; check the packet does not say glutinous. It keeps about a year airtight, but it clumps hard if it picks up damp, so sieve it before use and throw it out if it smells musty.

## Prep notes
- Whisk it into the dry flours before any water goes in, or it forms lumps that never break up.
- More rice flour means crisper but drier and more brittle, and it soaks up less frying oil. Past a third of the total flour the fritters start to taste of nothing.
