# Chili Lime Seasoning

A powder of mild chilli, dehydrated lime and a good deal of salt; Tajín is the reference brand. It reads as sour and gently warm rather than hot, and it is a finishing dust more than a cooking spice, over fruit, over cucumber, and over [[tex-mex-salad]]. Treat it as salt first: on most labels salt is the first ingredient, and the proportions vary between brands, so taste before adding any more [[kosher-salt]].

**Cuisines:** [[mexican]]

## Choosing & storing
Check which it is before you buy, as the chilli-lime seasonings sold as "salt-free" taste quite different and need salting separately. Keep it airtight and well away from steam, because the salt and citric acid set into a solid plug when damp and you end up chipping at it. The lime note is the first thing to fade, within about six months, after which it is mostly chilli and salt.

## Substitutes
- [[red-chili-powder]] with [[kosher-salt]] and [[lime]]: roughly one part chilli to one part salt, with lime juice squeezed on separately; close, though without citric acid it never dusts the same way
