# Avocado

Buttery, mild and faintly grassy. Its job in a salad is fat rather than flavour: it makes a bowl of raw vegetables feel like a meal and softens the need for a rich dressing. In both [[tex-mex-salad]] and [[quinoa-salad]] it goes in at the last moment, because the cut flesh browns within the hour once it meets air.

**Cuisines:** [[mexican]]

## Choosing & storing
Buy them firm and ripen them at home on the counter, which takes two to four days. Ready means it yields to gentle pressure in the palm (not the thumb, which bruises it) and the stem nub flicks off to show green underneath. Brown or black under that nub, or dark stringy patches inside, means it has gone too far. Once ripe, the fridge holds it for a couple of days, but a cold unripe avocado never ripens properly.

## Prep notes
Halve, twist out the stone, score and scoop, or dice it in the skin and spoon the cubes out. Cut it last, after everything else is in the bowl. A squeeze of [[lime]] slows the browning but does not stop it, so leftovers are a lost cause.
