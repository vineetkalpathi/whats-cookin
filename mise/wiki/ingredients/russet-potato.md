# Russet Potato

The big brown-skinned baking potato, high in starch and floury rather than waxy. It cooks soft and its edges fall apart, which thickens the liquid around it. That is exactly what is wanted in [[borscht]]: the cubes go in before the beets and cabbage, both because they take longest and because the starch they shed gives the soup its body. A waxy potato holds its shape and leaves the broth thin.

## Choosing & storing
Firm with dry, papery skin, no sprouts, no soft or weeping spots and no green under the skin. Keep them cool, dark and airy, in paper rather than plastic, and not in the fridge: cold converts the starch to sugar and they then brown oddly and taste sweet. Keep them away from onions, which make them sprout faster. Green skin means solanine, so peel it off generously or discard the potato.

## Prep notes
- Peel and cut just before cooking. Cut potato greys in air; hold it in cold water if there is a wait, and drain it well.
- Roughly 2 cm cubes so they are done at the same time as the beets.

## Substitutes
- Yukon gold or other waxy potato: 1:1, holds its shape, thickens the soup much less (no page yet)
