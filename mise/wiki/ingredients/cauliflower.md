# Cauliflower

A brassica grown for its unopened flower head: mild, faintly sweet, and sulphurous the moment it is overcooked. It takes on whatever it is cooked in, which is exactly its job in [[thai-curry]], where florets hold their shape in the coconut liquid and soak up the curry paste.

## Choosing & storing
A good head is heavy, tightly packed and creamy white, with crisp green leaves still wrapped round it; limp leaves are the first sign of age. Grey-brown speckling on the curd is oxidation and can be trimmed off, but a head that smells strongly or feels slimy between the florets is done. Keep it unwashed in a loose bag in the fridge for up to a week, and keep it dry, since trapped moisture is what starts the speckling.

## Prep notes
Quarter the head and cut the florets away from the core; the stalk is good eating sliced thin, so do not bin it. Cut the florets large, around four centimetres, if they are to simmer. Add it partway through so it has eight to ten minutes, no more. If the kitchen starts smelling of sulphur, it has already gone too far.

## Substitutes
- [[broccoli]]: 1:1, cooks faster and tastes distinctly greener
- [[frozen-peas]]: not a real swap, but it works if you only need something green and sweet in the curry
