# Vegetable Bouillon Paste

A salty savoury concentrate sold in a jar: a soft paste you spoon out and dissolve, usually a teaspoon to a cup of water. It is kept as a separate page from [[vegetable-broth]] because it is a separate purchase, a small jar in the fridge door rather than cartons of liquid in the cupboard, and the grocery list has to treat them differently. It generally tastes stronger and more rounded than a mid-range carton. Used as the alternative to broth in [[asparagus-soup]] and [[borscht]]. It is very salty, which is why both recipes say to salt late and lightly.

## Choosing & storing
Saltiness varies wildly between brands, so make the first batch up at half strength and taste before committing a whole pot to it. Check the label if it matters: some contain milk powder, and nearly all lean heavily on onion and garlic. Refrigerate after opening, where the salt keeps it good for months, though the surface darkens and dries.

## Prep notes
- Dissolve it in a mugful of hot water first rather than dropping the spoonful into the pot, where it sinks and scorches on the base.
- A teaspoon to 250 ml is the usual starting point, but check the jar; some are twice that strength.

## Substitutes
- [[vegetable-broth]]: 1 tsp paste in 1 cup hot water per cup of broth, then cut back on added salt
