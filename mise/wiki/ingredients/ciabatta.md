# Ciabatta

An open-crumbed Italian loaf with a thin, crisp crust and a chewy, holey interior. For a pressed sandwich like [[tomato-mozzarella-panini]] that structure is the point: it compresses without turning to paste, and the crust crisps and marks under the weight. The holes are also the weakness, since tomato juice and oil run straight through them, so oil the outside of the bread and keep the wet fillings off the cut faces.

**Cuisines:** [[italian]]

## Choosing & storing
Buy it the day you use it. A proper loaf feels light for its size, sounds hollow underneath and the crust cracks when you squeeze it. Supermarket part-baked rolls, including the torta rolls sold for sandwiches, are softer and denser, which matters less once pressed. It stales within a day and goes leathery rather than hard. Do not refrigerate it, as that stales it faster; freeze it sliced if it will not get eaten. Day-old ciabatta is better pressed or toasted than it is fresh.

## Prep notes
Split it horizontally and pull out a little of the crumb if the sandwich is too thick to press properly. Fat goes on the outside, not the inside.
