# Green Chutney

A fresh, uncooked chutney of [[cilantro]] and [[mint]] ground with [[green-chili]], ginger, lemon
and salt. Bright, herbal, sharp and hot — a condiment to spread or dip, not a sauce to cook with.
It goes on the paratha in [[paneer-kathi-rolls]], and in [[tomato-mozzarella-panini]] it stands in
for [[pesto]], which is what makes that sandwich an Indian-household panini rather than a caprese.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Jars from an [[indian-grocery]] are darker, saltier and more vinegary than fresh; homemade is much
better and takes five minutes in a blender. Either way it oxidises — fresh chutney goes from bright
green to olive within a day or two. Keep it airtight with the surface pressed flat and use it
within three or four days, or freeze it in ice-cube trays. Both recipes here treat it as bought or
already made, and give no quantities.

## Prep notes
Grind it with as little water as possible, and add the lemon juice at the end: the acid is what
holds the colour.

## Substitutes
- [[pesto]]: 1:1 in a sandwich — cheese-and-basil instead of herb-and-chili; [[tomato-mozzarella-panini]] treats the swap as working both ways
