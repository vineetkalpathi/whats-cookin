# Dried Red Chili

Whole dried red chilies, usually a long thin Indian variety. Dropped into hot fat for a few seconds
they darken, perfume the oil and give a clean background heat — which is a different thing from the
colour-and-sweetness of [[kashmiri-chili-powder]] or the coarse, pizza-shop bite of
[[red-pepper-flakes]]. Left whole and unbroken they are mild, because most of the heat is in the
seeds: [[yellow-moong-khichdi]] splits one in two over the pot so the seeds fall in, while
[[onion-tomato-chutney]] fries three or four whole with the dals. Mexican dried chilies (ancho,
guajillo) are the same idea but a different technique — toasted and soaked into a paste rather than
fried in a tadka.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]], [[mexican]]

## Choosing & storing
Good ones are still slightly flexible and deep red with a bit of gloss; brittle, dusty, brownish
chilies have lost both heat and aroma. Varieties differ enormously — Kashmiri and byadgi are mild,
guntur and the small round ones are fierce — so judge the count by the chilies you actually have
rather than by the number in a recipe. Keep them in a sealed jar out of the light and they last a
year or more; left loose in a humid kitchen they take up moisture and go soft or mouldy.

## Prep notes
Seconds in hot oil is all they need, and a blackened chili turns the whole tadka bitter. Leave them
whole for aroma with little heat; snap or split them to let the seeds out. They stay in the finished
dish, so pull them out before serving if someone is likely to bite into one.

## Substitutes
- [[red-pepper-flakes]]: about 1/4 tsp per chili, similar heat, none of the whole-chili aroma
- [[kashmiri-chili-powder]]: 1/2-1 tsp per chili, much more colour and far less heat
