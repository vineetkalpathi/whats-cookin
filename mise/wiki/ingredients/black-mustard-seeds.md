# Black Mustard Seeds

Small round black-brown seeds, pungent when crushed raw and nutty-sweet once they have popped in hot fat. They are the opening move of a tadka: in first, and they must sputter and crackle before [[cumin-seeds]] go in, because they need more heat than cumin and cumin burns in seconds. In [[yellow-moong-khichdi]] that order is what gives the dish its base note.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Whole seeds should be glossy and uniform, with little dust at the bottom of the bag. [[indian-grocery]] sells them in bags for a fraction of a supermarket spice jar, and whole they keep a year or more in a dry, airtight jar. Dullness, dust and broken seeds mean age; the pungency lives in the intact seed, so ground mustard is not worth keeping.

## Prep notes
Get the oil hot enough first. Drop one seed in: it should pop within a couple of seconds. Then add the rest and part-cover the pan, because they jump. Once the popping slows, move straight on to the next spice. Burnt mustard seeds go grey and acrid, and there is no rescuing the tadka, so start it again.
