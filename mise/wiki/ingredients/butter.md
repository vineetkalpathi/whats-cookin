# Butter

Churned cream, roughly 80% fat with water and milk solids making up the rest. Those solids are the whole difference from [[ghee]]: they brown, which is what you want in [[almond-flour-cake]] or against the crust of a [[tomato-mozzarella-panini]], and they burn at around 150C, which is why ghee is the fat for a tadka and butter is not. This repo buys the two separately. Unsalted is the default, so that salt stays something you add deliberately.

## Choosing & storing
Unsalted for baking; salted is fine for pressing a sandwich or finishing [[borscht]]. Foil-wrapped blocks keep better than tubs, as light and air are what turn butter rancid. Old butter tastes cheesy and faintly of crayon and the outer surface goes a darker yellow, which can be cut away if the inside still smells clean. It takes on fridge smells readily, so keep it wrapped and away from anything strong. It freezes well in its wrapper for months.

## Prep notes
"Softened" means it takes a fingerprint but is still cool and matte, which is thirty to sixty minutes out of the fridge. Melted or greasy-shiny butter will not hold air when you cream it, and the cake comes out dense.

## Substitutes
- [[ghee]]: about 7/8 the weight as a cooking fat, nuttier and far more heat-tolerant; not a straight swap in baking, where the water in butter matters
- [[neutral-oil]]: 1:1 by weight for frying, no dairy flavour and no browning
