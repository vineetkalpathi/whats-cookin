# Cumin Seeds

Whole cumin: warm, earthy, slightly bitter. The seeds are usually bloomed in hot fat until they darken a shade and crackle (see [[blooming-spices]]), which is the start of many Indian dishes. Dry-toasting and grinding them yourself gives a fresher, more aromatic powder than store-bought [[ground-cumin]]. Whole seeds keep their flavor for a year or more, much longer than ground.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Substitutes
- [[ground-cumin]]: about 1:1 by volume, but it can't be bloomed whole. Add it after the onions instead, or it scorches
- caraway seeds: 1/2 the amount, related but more anise-like (no page yet)
