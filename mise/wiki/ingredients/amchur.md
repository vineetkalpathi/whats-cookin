# Amchur

Unripe green mango, dried and ground to a pale, fibrous powder. It sours in a dusty, fruity way rather than with the clean sharpness of [[lime]], and crucially it sours without adding liquid, which is why it belongs in dry masalas and in the filling for [[paneer-kathi-rolls]]. A quarter-teaspoon reads as a lift; a whole teaspoon takes the dish over.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
It should be sandy and pale beige, and loose enough to pour. Humidity is the enemy: damp amchur darkens towards brown, cakes into a solid lump and loses its sourness, so it ends up adding nothing. Keep it in a tight jar away from the hob and replace it yearly. [[indian-grocery]] sells it fresher and far cheaper than a supermarket spice jar.

## Substitutes
- [[lime]]: a squeeze per 1/2 tsp, brighter and cleaner, but it adds liquid
- [[tamarind]]: 1 tsp paste per 1/2 tsp, darker and sweeter, and it wets the mix
