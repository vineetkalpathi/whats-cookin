# Jalapeño

The fresh green chili: firm, thick-walled and bright, with a grassy, vegetal heat that is moderate
and genuinely unpredictable — some are nearly sweet, some are hot. Sliced raw over
[[tex-mex-salad]] it gives heat and crunch at the same time. Fresh is a different ingredient from
[[pickled-jalapenos]], which trade the grassiness for vinegar and softness, and different again
from the dried smoked version sold as chipotle.

**Cuisines:** [[mexican]]

## Choosing & storing
Firm, glossy and heavy, with no soft spots. Fine pale lines on the skin (corking) usually mean a
more mature, hotter pepper, and a red jalapeño is simply a ripe one — sweeter and often hotter.
Loose in the fridge drawer they keep a couple of weeks and shrivel slowly. Freeze any surplus
whole: fine for cooking, useless for slicing raw.

## Prep notes
The heat sits in the white pith and the seeds, so scrape them out for a milder result and leave
them in for a hot one. Slice crossways into rounds for a garnish. Taste a sliver before committing
a whole pepper — the variation between peppers is bigger than the difference between one and two.

## Substitutes
- [[green-chili]]: about half to one Indian green chili per jalapeño, thinner-walled and hotter
- [[pickled-jalapenos]]: 1:1 by volume, vinegary and soft, with none of the raw crunch
