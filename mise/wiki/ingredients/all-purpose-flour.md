# All-Purpose Flour

Plain wheat flour of middling protein, between cake and bread flour. It does two separate jobs here: a spoonful as a thickener (1 tsp in [[asparagus-soup]]) and structure in the batters for [[methi-lagaan]] and [[killu-bonda]], where it sits alongside [[besan]]. Unlike besan it is bland and forms gluten, so it binds and sets rather than adding flavour. On an Indian packet it is sold as maida.

## Choosing & storing
Any supermarket bag is fine; sift or whisk it if it has sat long enough to clump. Keep it airtight and cool. Flour left in a paper bag in a warm cupboard picks up weevils (fine brown dust and webbing in the corners are the sign) and goes musty, and a musty smell carries straight into whatever you bake. It keeps six to eight months, far longer than [[whole-wheat-flour]], which has the germ in it and turns rancid.

## Prep notes
For thickening, either cook the flour in the fat for a minute first or slurry it with cold liquid. Tipped dry into hot liquid it seizes into lumps that never disperse. In batters, mix only until it comes together.

## Substitutes
- [[whole-wheat-flour]]: 1:1, heavier and nuttier, and it drinks more liquid so slacken the batter
- cornflour: half the quantity for thickening only, clearer and glossier, no raw-flour taste to cook out (no page yet)
