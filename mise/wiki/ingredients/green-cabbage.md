# Green Cabbage

The dense, pale-green head cabbage. Raw it is crunchy and faintly peppery; cooked it turns sweet
and soft. It is sturdy enough to sit in a dressing without collapsing and cheap enough to use by
the half-head. It behaves much like [[red-cabbage]], and [[asian-cabbage-salad]] uses the two
together mainly for colour, but green holds up better in a long simmer, which is its job in
[[borscht]]. [[killu-bonda]] uses it shredded raw, straight into the batter.

**Cuisines:** [[russian-ukrainian]]

## Choosing & storing
Pick a head that is heavy for its size and tightly packed, with a pale, moist-looking cut stem — a
dry, split or browning stem end means it has been sitting. Outer leaves should be firm rather than
floppy. A whole head keeps for weeks in the fridge drawer. Once cut, wrap the cut face and use it
within a week, trimming the dried edge off before you slice.

## Prep notes
Quarter it, cut out the dense core, and slice across the grain. [[asian-cabbage-salad]] is
emphatic about doing this by hand from a whole head: bagged slaw works in a pinch, but the source
says it "does not compare". Salting shredded cabbage for twenty minutes and squeezing it out gives
a softer, less watery slaw, at the cost of the crunch.

## Substitutes
- [[red-cabbage]]: 1:1, same texture, bleeds purple into everything around it
- savoy cabbage: 1:1, looser and more tender, better cooked than raw (no page yet)
