# Baking Soda

Pure sodium bicarbonate. It is alkaline, so it only raises when there is acid in the batter (yoghurt, buttermilk, lemon, sour fruit); without one you get little lift and a soapy, metallic aftertaste. It is three or four times stronger than [[baking-powder]], which is why quantities are a pinch or a quarter-teaspoon. In [[killu-bonda]] and [[methi-lagaan]] that pinch keeps the batter open rather than heavy.

## Choosing & storing
Cheap and long-lasting if kept dry, so buy the small box rather than the catering tub. It absorbs smells, which is the one real trap: the box that has been deodorising the fridge is not for baking. Test it with a pinch in vinegar, which should fizz hard immediately.

## Prep notes
Add it to the dry ingredients, mix briefly and cook soon after. Once it meets the acid the reaction starts, and a batter left standing has already spent most of its lift.

## Substitutes
- [[baking-powder]]: 3 tsp per 1 tsp soda, brings its own acid, but that much extra powder affects the taste and salt balance
