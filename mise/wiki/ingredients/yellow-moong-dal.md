# Yellow Moong Dal

Split, skinned mung bean: small, flat and pale yellow. The gentlest of the dals, needing no soak, cooking to a soft purée in 20 to 25 minutes, and tasting mild and faintly sweet, which is why it is the dal cooked for anyone unwell. Equal parts with rice in [[yellow-moong-khichdi]]. In [[tomato-soup]] it is dry-roasted first and then cooked right down, where it does the thickening instead of cream. Compare [[urad-dal]], starchier and almost gluey and used by the spoonful as a seasoning, and [[chana-dal]], which is bigger, firmer, takes far longer and keeps its shape.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Look for an even pale yellow. Dull, grey-tinged dal is old, tastes flat and takes longer to break down. Airtight in the cupboard it keeps up to a year. Rinse it in two or three changes of water until the water stops going cloudy.

## Prep notes
- Dry-roasting it in a pan until it smells nutty and darkens a shade deepens the flavour a great deal and helps it hold a little texture. This is what [[tomato-soup]] wants, and it is worth doing in khichdi too.
- It foams heavily as it comes to the boil, so leave headroom in the pot or skim, and keep the lid off a pressure cooker vent clear.
