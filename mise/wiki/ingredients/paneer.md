# Paneer

Fresh unaged cheese, made by curdling milk with acid and pressing it: mild, milky, faintly squeaky,
and it does not melt. That is what it is for — it keeps its shape in a hot pan and takes on
whatever it has been marinated in. In [[paneer-kathi-rolls]] it sits in a spiced yogurt marinade
and then gets only 3-4 minutes in the skillet, deliberately, because longer tightens it into
rubber.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Sold in vacuum-packed blocks at an [[indian-grocery]], refrigerated or frozen. It should be white
and uniform; yellowing or a sour smell means it is past it. Once opened it dries out fast — keep
the unused piece submerged in water in a sealed box, change the water daily, and use it within
three or four days. Frozen blocks keep for months but come out firmer and more crumbly; thaw them
in the fridge.

## Prep notes
Cube it, then soak the cubes in warm lightly salted water for ten to twenty minutes if the block
feels firm; it softens them noticeably. Pat dry before it goes into a marinade or a hot pan. Short
and hot is the only way to cook it — it is already cooked, and all the pan is doing is setting the
spices.

## Substitutes
- [[extra-firm-tofu]]: 1:1, pressed and drained; takes a marinade the same way and makes the dish vegan, with less richness
- [[queso-fresco]]: 1:1, close in texture, but salted — cut the salt elsewhere in the recipe
