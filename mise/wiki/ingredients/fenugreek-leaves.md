# Fenugreek Leaves

Fresh methi: small clover-shaped leaves, deeply savoury and frankly bitter, with the maple-ish
aroma fenugreek is known for. Cooking tames the bitterness without removing it, and that is the
point of the vegetable. This is the fresh green, used in cupfuls — [[methi-lagaan]] takes 1 1/2
cups chopped — where [[kasoori-methi]] is the same plant dried and used by the tablespoon as a
finishing aroma. At those quantities they are not interchangeable.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Sold in bunches at an [[indian-grocery]], usually sandy. Pick bunches with small, dry, unwilted
leaves; large coarse leaves are more bitter and their stems are woody. Wash it well, because it
holds grit, then dry it thoroughly and keep it wrapped in a box — three or four days at most. A
bunch yields far less than it looks once the leaves are off the stems. Frozen chopped methi is a
legitimate shortcut, and the source behind [[methi-lagaan]] accepts fresh or frozen outright.

## Prep notes
Pick the leaves off the thicker stems; the stems are stringy and the most bitter part. If the bunch
is coarse, salting the chopped leaves for ten minutes and squeezing out the liquid takes the edge
off the bitterness. Young leaves do not need it.

## Substitutes
- [[spinach]]: 1:1 by volume, no bitterness and no fenugreek aroma; palak is the stated alternative in [[methi-lagaan]]
- [[kasoori-methi]]: not a volume swap — dried is far more concentrated, so a spoonful adds the aroma but cannot stand in for a cup of greens
