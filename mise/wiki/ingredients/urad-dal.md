# Urad Dal

Split and skinned black gram: small, flat, ivory-coloured. Starchier and more mucilaginous than other dals, so cooked long it goes creamy and almost gluey, which is why it is the dal in idli and dosa batter. Here it is used as a seasoning rather than a pulse: a spoonful fried in hot oil alongside [[chana-dal]] until golden, then ground, giving [[onion-tomato-chutney]] both its body and a toasty, nutty background. Compare [[chana-dal]], which is bigger and firmer and stays sweet and nutty, and [[yellow-moong-dal]], the smallest and softest of the three.

**Cuisines:** [[indian]]

## Choosing & storing
Buy the split skinned kind, often labelled dhuli urad, not whole black urad, which has its skin on and behaves differently. Airtight in the cupboard it keeps up to a year. Old dal smells flat and takes noticeably longer to cook, and like all dals it attracts weevils, so check a bag before buying in quantity.

## Prep notes
- Fry it on medium heat and do not look away. It goes pale, then golden, then bitter and black within seconds, and burnt dal ruins the whole chutney.
- Pull the pan off the heat while it is still a shade lighter than you want; the residual oil heat takes it the rest of the way.
