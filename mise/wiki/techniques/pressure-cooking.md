# Pressure Cooking

Cooking in a sealed pot where steam pressure raises water's boiling point to about 118-120°C (245°F). The hotter water softens dense foods like dried beans in a fraction of the stovetop time.

## How to
1. Build flavor first on sauté if the pot allows (tadka, onions), then add liquid and scrape the bottom clean.
2. Seal, set the time on high pressure.
3. **Natural release** for beans and legumes: letting pressure drop on its own keeps skins intact and finishes the cooking gently.

## Timings (high pressure, natural release)
| Food | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Soaked dried chickpeas | 35 min | [[traditional-chana-masala-recipe-chole]], [[instant-pot-chana-masala-punjabi-chole]] |
| Canned chickpeas in a curry (½ cup water) | 5 min | [[instant-pot-chana-masala-punjabi-chole]] |

## Doneness cues
Chickpeas should crush easily between two fingers. If not, seal and cook 5-10 minutes more.

## Common mistakes
- Browned bits stuck to the bottom: the Instant Pot reads it as burning.
- Adding tomatoes or acid before pressure cooking can keep beans firm; the Instant Pot chana masala adds tomatoes after. (per [[instant-pot-chana-masala-punjabi-chole]])
- Too little liquid: most pots need about ½-1 cup minimum to come to pressure.
