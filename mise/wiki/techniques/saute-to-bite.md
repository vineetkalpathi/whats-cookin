# Sautéing Vegetables to a Chosen Bite

Cooking a pan of mixed vegetables to a texture you pick deliberately - crunchy, or soft - by controlling heat and whether the pan is covered. Both of Amma's vegetable recipes ([[bean-filling]], [[veggie-pasta]]) state this as an explicit choice rather than leaving it to chance, which is what makes it worth a page.

## Why it works
Vegetables are mostly water. As they heat, that water comes out. What happens to it decides the texture:

- **High heat, uncovered:** water evaporates as fast as it is released. The pan stays hot, surfaces brown, and the vegetables stop cooking before their interiors go soft. Result: crunch.
- **Lower heat, or a lid on:** water stays in the pan and the vegetables effectively steam in it. The interiors cook through. Result: soft.

The lid is the stronger lever of the two - covering a pan converts a saute into a braise.

## Steps
1. Heat about 3 tbsp oil in a wide [[skillet]].
2. Add the garlic. After 3-5 seconds - long enough to smell it, not long enough to colour it - add the onion.
3. When the onion turns slightly translucent, add the rest of the vegetables, washed.
4. Now choose: high heat and uncovered for crunch, lower heat or a lid for softness.
5. Season at the end, once the vegetables are where you want them.

## When it's done
Taste a piece of the slowest vegetable, usually carrot. The dish is over when that one is right; everything else will be softer.

## Common mistakes
- **A crowded pan.** Too many vegetables at once means the water can't evaporate, so you get soft vegetables whether you wanted them or not. Use a wider pan or cook in batches.
- **Wet vegetables.** Drain properly after washing; the water goes in the pan otherwise.
- **Seasoning early.** Salt pulls water out, which works against a crunchy result. Both source recipes season at the end.
- **Everything in at once** when you want distinct textures - carrot and broccoli need longer than zucchini and mushroom.
