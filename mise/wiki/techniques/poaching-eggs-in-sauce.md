# Poaching Eggs in Sauce

Cracking eggs directly into a simmering sauce and letting them set there, instead of poaching them in water and adding them later. The sauce seasons the egg and the egg enriches the sauce. [[shakshuka]] is the canonical example; eggs in curry, in tomato sauce, and in beans all work the same way.

## Why it works
A sauce holds heat more evenly than water and moves less, so the white sets where you put it rather than drifting. The sauce has to be thick enough to hold a depression - that is the whole precondition. In water, poaching depends on vinegar and a vortex to keep the white together; here the sauce does that job by simply being viscous.

## Steps
1. Reduce the sauce until it mounds behind a spoon. A thin sauce will swallow the eggs and you'll get egg-drop soup.
2. Take the pan off the heat for a moment so the surface is still.
3. Make a well per egg with the back of a spoon, pushing the sauce aside down to the pan. Space them out - wells that touch merge.
4. Crack each egg into its well (into a cup first, if you want to be careful).
5. Cover and cook on medium-low. Low and covered is the point: the steam sets the tops while the bottoms cook in the sauce.

## When it's done
The whites are opaque and just firm, the yolks still wobble. Pull the pan a touch early - the eggs keep cooking in the hot sauce after the heat is off, and that residual heat is what ruins yolks.

## Common mistakes
- **Sauce too thin.** The single biggest one. Reduce it first.
- **Heat too high.** The sauce spits, the bottom of the egg turns rubbery before the top sets.
- **No lid.** The tops stay raw while the bases overcook.
- **Crowding.** Eggs touching each other fuse into one sheet.
