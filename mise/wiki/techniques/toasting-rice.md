# Toasting Rice

Frying dry rice in fat until it turns golden before any liquid goes in. The step that separates pilaf, Mexican rice, and risotto from plain boiled rice.

## Why it works
Two things happen at once. The grains brown, which builds nutty, popcorn-like flavour that boiling can never produce. And each grain gets coated in fat, which slows water absorption at the surface, so the grains stay distinct instead of knitting into a mass.

## Steps
1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then **drain it well**. Wet rice steams instead of browning - this is the step people skip.
2. Heat the fat. In an [[instant-pot]], use saute mode; otherwise a wide [[skillet]] or heavy pan.
3. Add the rice and stir often, keeping it in a single moving layer.
4. Toast until lightly golden and smelling nutty, about 5 minutes.
5. Add aromatics and liquid, and cook as the recipe says ([[pressure-cooking]] in the case of [[instant-pot-mexican-rice]]).

## When it's done
Pale gold, not brown, with a toasted smell. Some grains will look chalky white - that is normal.

## Common mistakes
- **Not draining after rinsing.** The rice steams and never colours.
- **Too little fat.** [[instant-pot-mexican-rice]] uses 1/4 cup oil for 1 1/2 cups rice, which looks like a lot and isn't.
- **Walking away.** It goes from golden to burnt quickly, and burnt rice taints the whole pot.
