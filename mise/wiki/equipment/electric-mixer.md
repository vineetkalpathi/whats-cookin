# Electric Mixer

A hand mixer, or a stand mixer with a whisk attachment. The job it does here is whipping egg
whites to soft peaks and beating sugar into them for [[almond-flour-cake]]
([[folding-egg-whites]]). A balloon whisk and a few minutes of arm work will do the same, but the
usual failure by hand is stopping short of a stable foam, and in a flourless cake the foam is the
only leavening there is. The same mixer beats the yolks, sugar and vanilla smooth.

## Care
- Wash and dry the beaters immediately. Egg white dries to a varnish that is much harder to shift
  later.
- Keep the beaters and the bowl free of fat. Any grease, or a trace of yolk, stops whites foaming
  properly, so wipe a doubtful bowl with a little vinegar or lemon juice and dry it.
- Wipe the motor housing rather than rinsing it, and unplug before changing beaters.

## Buying notes
- A hand mixer is enough for everything in this wiki. A stand mixer only earns its counter space
  with bread dough and larger batches.
- Check it comes with a proper wire whisk as well as flat beaters. The whisk is what builds volume
  in whites; wattage barely matters for this.
- Variable speed with a genuinely low setting is worth having: you want slow at the start of
  whipping and when the sugar goes in.
- Use a deep, narrow bowl rather than a wide one, so the beaters stay buried and the whites climb.
