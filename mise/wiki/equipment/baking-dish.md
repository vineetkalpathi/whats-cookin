# Baking Dish

An oven-safe rectangular or square dish - glass, ceramic or metal - for anything baked in a layer
rather than in a tin: gratins, traybakes, and savoury batters like [[methi-lagaan]], which is
poured in greased, levelled off, and scattered with roasted [[sesame-seeds]]
([[dry-roasting]]) before it goes in.

## Care
- Grease it properly for savoury batters. Semolina and besan stick hard, and a lagaan that tears
  coming out is almost always an under-greased dish.
- Glass and ceramic dislike sudden temperature changes. Don't set a hot dish on a wet surface or
  run cold water into it, and keep them out from under the broiler.
- Soak baked-on edges rather than scouring; a scratched glaze only sticks worse next time.

## Buying notes
- A 9x13 and an 8-inch square cover almost everything. The 8-inch square is the better match for
  a half batch of a batter bake.
- Glass lets you see whether the base is browning, which is useful when a batter looks set on top
  and is still raw underneath.
- Metal conducts faster and browns harder than glass or ceramic, so the same batter bakes quicker
  in it. Check 5 minutes early if you swap.
- Straight sides and square corners give even portions; sloped sides overbake the edges.
