# Slow Cooker

A countertop crock that holds a low, steady heat for hours, so a pot can be left alone all day.
Not owned here. It appears as the alternative method in
[[thai-coconut-butternut-squash-soup]]: squash, aromatics, curry paste, broth and seasoning go in
raw with no oil at all, and cook on low for 6-8 hours or high for 3-4.

The trade-off is worth knowing. Nothing browns in a slow cooker, so the curry paste is never
fried ([[frying-curry-paste]]) and the soup loses the depth that stage builds; and because the lid
stays on, almost no liquid evaporates, so a slow-cooker version comes out thinner than a stovetop
one. What you get in return is a pot you can ignore.

## If you don't own one
- A heavy [[dutch-oven]] on the lowest burner setting, lid on, is the direct equivalent, though it
  wants an occasional look.
- The [[instant-pot]] has a slow-cook mode and does this job in the pot you already have.
- For this soup in particular, the stovetop route is the easier answer: it simmers in about 20
  minutes and it lets you fry the paste first.

## Buying notes
- Only worth buying if you want unattended cooking. Everything here can be done without one.
- A 6-quart oval suits a whole squash or a large soup; smaller crocks scorch at the edges when
  under-filled, since they are sized to be two-thirds full.
- A ceramic insert that lifts out makes it washable and lets you chill leftovers in it. Treat it
  like any ceramic dish and avoid sudden temperature changes.
