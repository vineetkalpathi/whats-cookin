# Panini Press

A hinged double-sided grill: two heated plates, usually ridged, that toast both faces of a
sandwich at once while the weight of the upper plate presses it flat. That is the whole appeal -
the bread crisps on the outside while the trapped heat softens the filling, with no flipping and
no guessing. Here it makes [[tomato-mozzarella-panini]], where the bread is buttered on the
outside and the pesto or green chutney stays inside.

## Care
- Wipe the plates while the press is still warm, with a damp cloth and a wooden or plastic
  scraper. Melted cheese sets like glue once it cools.
- Nothing metal on coated plates, and never submerge the body of the press.
- Buttering the bread rather than oiling the plates keeps the plates much cleaner, and it is what
  the source does anyway.
- Catch the drip tray if it has one; cheese that runs out and bakes on is the usual source of
  smoke next time.

## If you don't own one
A heavy skillet over medium heat with a second heavy pan set on top of the sandwich does the same
job: toast one side, flip, press again. The source says as much. You lose the grill marks and the
simultaneous toasting, not the result. A foil-wrapped brick or a filled kettle works as the
weight.

## Buying notes
- A floating or adjustable hinge is the feature that matters. A fixed hinge presses a thick
  ciabatta hard at the front and barely touches the back.
- Ridged plates give the stripes and let fat drain; flat plates are more useful if you also want
  to use it as a general griddle.
- Removable plates are much easier to wash than a fixed grill.
- A temperature control helps with fillings that need time to melt, such as thick slices of fresh
  mozzarella; a single preset runs hot and crisps the bread before the cheese has given way.
