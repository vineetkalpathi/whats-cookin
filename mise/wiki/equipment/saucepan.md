# Saucepan

A deep, straight-sided pan with a lid, 2-4 quarts. It is the pan for anything with more liquid
than a [[skillet]] can hold: boiling grains and pasta, warming beans, simmering a small curry.
In this collection it cooks the quinoa for [[quinoa-salad]] and [[tex-mex-salad]], the pasta for
[[pasta-salad]], and it is the pan [[thai-curry]] is built in - aromatics and paste fried in the
base ([[frying-curry-paste]]), then the coconut milk poured straight in on top.

## Care
- Starch sticks. Soak a pasta or quinoa pan rather than scraping it; the film lifts off in
  minutes.
- A scorched base from frying a curry paste responds to water and a simmer, not to abrasives.
- Dry the base before it goes back on the heat if the pan is clad; trapped water under a warped
  base makes it rock.

## Buying notes
- If you own one, make it 3 quarts: big enough for a pound of pasta at a push, small enough to
  boil half a cup of quinoa without scorching.
- A thick, heat-spreading base is the thing worth paying for. A thin one has hot spots, and a
  curry paste burns in them before the rest of the pan is hot.
- A tight lid matters more than you would think: grains are cooked by absorption, so steam that
  escapes is water the recipe assumed.
- Tall and narrow holds liquid well but is slow to reduce. Wide and shallow is the opposite.
