# Cake Pan

A round tin, 8 or 9 inches, for cakes that are turned out rather than served from the dish.
[[almond-flour-cake]] uses an 8-inch one, greased with melted butter and then dusted with 2 tbsp
of the recipe's sugar, which bakes into a thin crisp crust on what becomes the top once the cake
is inverted. The batter is a whipped-white foam ([[folding-egg-whites]]), so the pan needs to be
ready and greased before the whites are folded in.

## Care
- Wash by hand and dry straight away. Dishwasher detergent dulls non-stick coatings, and bare
  steel or aluminium spots and rusts if left wet.
- To release a cake, run something thin round the edge - a palette knife or a plastic bowl
  scraper, not a sharp metal knife if the pan is coated.
- Don't store anything acidic in it. Bare aluminium reacts and takes on a grey cast.

## Buying notes
- One 8-inch round is enough for this collection. The almond cake is keyed to that size.
- Light-coloured aluminium bakes more evenly than dark non-stick, which absorbs more heat and can
  over-brown the crust before the middle sets.
- Straight sides, not sloped, if you ever want to stack layers.
- A loose base or a springform is kinder to a delicate, egg-leavened sponge than inverting it,
  though the almond cake is sturdy enough to turn out.
- Going from an 8-inch to a 9-inch spreads the same batter thinner: it bakes faster and comes out
  flatter, so start checking well before the stated time.
